5(a) Risk management process and risk register format

Risk management process followed

We followed Sommerville's four-step risk management cycle [1] from the ENG1 risk lecture [2], scaled appropriately for a 6-week, 7-person student game project:

  1. Risk Identification: In Week 2 (and reviewed each week), we listed practical risks across six standard areas [1, 2]: Schedule/Project, People/Team, Requirements/Scope, Architecture/Integration, Technology/Platform, and Legal/Licensing.
  2. Risk Analysis (Qualitative Scales): Because this is a short desktop game project with no past failure data, exact percentage probabilities would be guesswork [2]. Following the module guidance, we scored Likelihood and Impact on simple three-level scales (Low / Medium / High) to rank overall Exposure:
    LevelLikelihood (L)Impact (I) if the risk happens
    LowUnlikely over the 6-week project (<20%).Half a day or less of extra work; absorbed without losing marks.
    MediumRealistic in a student group project (20–50%).Delays a work package by 1–3 days or reduces non-essential polish.
    HighLikely without a clear plan (>50%).Threatens a submission deadline, page limit, licence rule or runnable JAR.
    Exposure rule: High × High = Critical; High × Med or Med × High = High; Med × Med or Low × High = Medium; otherwise Low.
  3. Risk Planning (Reducing Impact When Risks Happen): Following [1, 3] and ENG1 Section 4.8, risks like illness, diverging code branches or driver issues cannot be prevented just by "trying harder". Each Mitigation entry in our register therefore sets out practical steps that limit the damage and keep us moving if the risk occurs (such as backup reviewers, isolating OpenGL in screen/, dropping optional visual extras, or keeping fallback builds).
  4. Risk Monitoring: High-exposure risks were checked alongside our weekly plan snapshots, updating each status (Active, Triggered & Mitigated, or Retired) up to submission.

Justification of risk register format

  • Clear IDs and categories (ID, Category): Fixed IDs (R01–R12) let our weekly plan snapshots refer directly to specific risks, while categories check that we covered team, schedule and legal risks as well as code bugs [1, 2].
  • Cause and effect in one column (Risk event & project effect): Writing both what might happen and how it hurts the project avoids vague labels like "Git problems" and justifies the Impact score [2].
  • Simple L / I / Exposure ratings: Easy to scan quickly in weekly meetings without complex spreadsheets.
  • Concrete Mitigation actions: Focuses on fallback plans and damage limitation if triggered.
  • One named Owner and live Status: Every risk has one named person watching it (never "All") so someone is clearly responsible [2], and the status column records which mitigations actually had to be used in Weeks 5–6.

Summary at Assessment 1 submission

Four of our 12 tracked risks occurred during Weeks 5–6 (R02 separate prototype branches, R07 slow-frame timing/collision edge cases, R09 transitive LGPL audio licences in the fat JAR, and R10 draft page-count overflow) and were resolved using the planned mitigations above without missing our schedule. Remaining pre-submission checks (R04 and R11) are covered by our packaging script and release checklist.
