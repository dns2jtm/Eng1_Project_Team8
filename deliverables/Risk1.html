5(a) Risk management process and risk register format

Following Sommerville [1] and the ENG1 risk lecture [2], we grouped candidate risks into four main types: Project (schedule, people, coordination), Product (game quality, performance, libraries), Project & Product, and Business (commercial marketability for the customer plus Assessment 2 handover).

Our team followed a four-step iterative risk management process [1, 3] across Weeks 2–7:

  1. Risk identification: Brainstormed instabilities and failure points across team availability, requirements interpretation, Git integration, cross-platform Java execution and licensing.
  2. Risk analysis (simple qualitative scales): Because ENG1 builds non-critical software with no historical failure dataset, numeric probabilities would be guesswork [2]. We rated Likelihood and Severity (Impact) on simple three-level scales (Low, Medium, High):
    RatingLikelihoodSeverity (impact if risk occurs)
    LowUnlikely during the 6-week schedule.Minor rework (≤0.5 day); absorbed cleanly.
    MediumPlausible in a 7-student coursework team.Delays a task by 1–3 days or trims polish.
    HighLikely without active coordination.Threatens deadlines, page limits, JAR or marks.
  3. Risk planning (mitigation and damage control): Per [1, 3] and Section 4.8, risks like illness or OS quirks cannot simply be "prevented". Each mitigation specifies concrete impact-reduction and fallback steps if the risk occurs (task shadowing, isolated review branches, keeping OpenGL out of domain logic, tunable GameConfig constants).
  4. Risk monitoring: Elliott and Dawud reviewed the register alongside weekly plan snapshots (planning.html) to act early before wider repercussions developed.

Justification of risk register format

  • Risk ID & Type: Stable IDs (R01–R11) link from weekly snapshots; Sommerville's four types [1] ensure non-code risks are covered.
  • Description & effect: Pairs the trigger with its effect on deliverables or Req1 IDs to justify Severity [2].
  • Colour-coded Likelihood & Severity: Fast to scan in weekly meetings without complex spreadsheets.
  • Mitigation method & named Ownership: Assigns concrete fallback steps to specific named members (never "All") for clear accountability [2].
