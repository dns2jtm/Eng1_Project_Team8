2(a) Elicitation, negotiation and presentation

Stakeholders. Direct: the customer (a module lecturer who may market and sell the game) and players; the customer defined the target player as a 16-year-old student interested in computer science. Indirect: the ENG1 team that will inherit and extend the product in Assessment 2.

Elicitation. (1) Document analysis [1, 4]: we broke the product brief and assessment paper into candidate requirements and split them into Assessment 1 and Assessment 2 scope. (2) A semi-structured interview with the customer using prepared questions on ambiguous points in the brief (questionnaires and workplace observation [1] were ruled out because one customer sets the brief and no legacy system exists):

QuestionCustomer answerRequirements affected
What counts as a hidden event? A triggered activity changing game state; "hidden" means consequences are unknown beforehand; story stays consistent; no marks for creativity alone. FR_EVENT_HIDDEN, FR_EVENT_TRIGGER, FR_EVENT_EFFECT
How should geese behave? They should stop the player moving freely ("bullet hell" style crowding pressure). FR_GEESE_SPAWN, FR_GEESE_BLOCK
Multiple difficulties? The game must be easy to play and short; a harder difficulty is optional. UR_CASUAL, QR_LEARNABILITY
Graphics preference? 2D top-down is fine. CR_2D_TOPDOWN
Target audience? A 16-year-old student interested in computer science. UR_CASUAL, QR_LEARNABILITY, QR_MAP_CLARITY
Where on campus? An open campus area. UR_CAMPUS_MAP, FR_CAMPUS_THEME

Negotiation. Disagreements within the team were settled against the brief and customer answers [1, 4]:

  • A harder difficulty mode was left out because the customer confirmed it was optional and the brief discourages unjustified features (KISS).
  • Subjective goals ("fun", "casual", "winnable") were turned into measurable Volere fit criteria [3] (QR_LEARNABILITY, QR_MAP_CLARITY, QR_BALANCE) to be checked in the Assessment 2 user evaluation; the win threshold in FR_WIN_CONDITION is tunable via play-testing.
  • Every system requirement is tagged A1 or A2 to match the reduced Assessment 1 scope.

Presentation. Following the requirements lecture [4], Sommerville [1] and ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148 [2]:

  • Two levels: user requirements (UR_) use plain language for the customer; system requirements (FR_, QR_, CR_) specify testable developer obligations [1].
  • Meaningful IDs: topic prefixes stay stable if rows are added or reordered and match identifiers across UML diagrams, Java classes and tests (traceability.html) [2].
  • Verifiability: system requirements link back to user requirements, quality requirements include fit criteria [3], and priorities use Shall (mandatory) and Should (desirable) [2].
  • Tables, not use cases: continuous real-time rules fit tables more clearly and concisely than textual use-case forms.

2(b) User and system requirements

Single statement of need: A short, casual, single-player 2D game in which a UoY student escapes a herd of hungry geese on campus within 5 minutes by using items wisely and dealing with hidden events (full traceability matrix on traceability.html).

UR_WIN_LOSEThe game shall end in victory once enough geese are dispersed, and in defeat if time runs out.Shall

UR_PROGRESS_FEEDBACKThe player shall be able to see how many items they have used and events they have interacted with.Shall
UR_CASUALThe game shall be accessible and enjoyable for a casual audience.Should
